# MathCups Mathematical Archipelago: Semantic Specification v1

This atlas organizes mathematical concepts, objects, theorems, methods, and problems with identifiable sources into islands, and relations with explicit meanings into routes. It is a verifiable layer for organizing knowledge and navigating research. Proximity in two dimensions, links within reference sources, and mathematical transferability are three different quantities.

## 1. What counts as an island

An island is a mathematical knowledge entry with a stable identifier in a source. Redirects, aliases, capitalization differences, and Unicode normalization differences within the same source do not increase the count. People, institutions, bibliographies, timelines, indexes, category pages, and empty placeholder pages cannot be counted as mathematical concept islands. If proof entries, examples, or exercises are retained, they must carry their own content types rather than being presented as distinct concepts.

Identical names across sources do not automatically establish identity: one name can refer to different objects, and different names can be synonymous. Merging must preserve the original IDs, source titles, aliases, and the reason for the merge. “More than 10,000” refers to the number of nodes after rule-based deduplication. It does not mean that mathematicians have individually reviewed the semantics of every entry, or that mathematical knowledge has been exhausted. Use `unclassified` when the island type is undetermined; do not assign `theorem` to large batches by guessing.

Recommended node fields are `id`, `title`, `aliases`, `kind`, `domains`, `sources`, `source_ids`, `deduplication`, `typed_annotations`, and `review_status`. Island types may be `concept/object/theorem/method/problem/proof/example/unclassified`. Domains may have multiple values; a single color used in the layout does not change this multivalued meaning.

An implementation may preserve finer source types such as `result/corollary/axiom/conjecture/theory/property/tool/research_program/correspondence/field/framework`. Do not erase the original `source_kind` merely to satisfy a smaller enumeration. These classify source content; they are not the functional tool types of MathCups. The current first release uses `label` for the title, `source_url/source_id` together with source-level manifests to connect entries to their sources, and `domain/domain_code` for a single cartographic grouping. It does not yet provide a complete, manually formalized account of every island's domains and tool contracts. Independent source types and unassigned MathCups type fields should both be retained.

## 2. Route semantics and evidence

| Relation type | Meaning | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `encyclopedic_link` | The body or a relation field of source entry A explicitly points to B. | A's proof depends on B; B is applicable to A. |
| `classification` | A source assigns an entry to a category. | Every pair of members of the category admits a mathematical transformation. |
| `equivalence` | A bidirectional correspondence holds within the specified range of objects and axioms. | The correspondence remains valid after arbitrarily enlarging that range. |
| `representation` | A problem is expressed using another kind of object. | The representation is lossless, reversible, or computable, unless each property is proved separately. |
| `reduction` | A reduction holds in the stated direction and within the specified complexity bounds. | A reverse reduction, or automatic preservation of algorithmic efficiency. |
| `construction` | A mathematical input is used to construct another kind of object. | Every input satisfies the construction's prerequisites. |
| `bound_transfer` | An invariant or estimate controls another quantity. | Equality or optimality. |
| `invariant_identity` | Two invariants satisfy an identity within the specified scope. | A reversible correspondence between the original objects, or complete classification by that invariant. |
| `proof_dependency` | A step in a specified proof invokes another theorem. | Every proof depends on that result. |
| `candidate_bridge` | A proposed transfer, generated by an explicit mechanism, awaits validation. | A new theorem, an independent discovery, or novelty. |

Edges must include at least `id/source/target/relation/status/evidence`. Mathematical bridges also include `mechanism/assumptions/conclusion/direction/limitations`. Store evidence states separately: whether the source can be located, whether the relevant passage has been read, whether the mathematical statement has been reviewed, and whether a formal proof exists. Do not compress these states into a single uncalibrated “confidence score.” `known_in_literature` means only that the statement appears in existing literature; it never means that the atlas builders have proved it again.

Semantic-similarity edges without evidence and shared-classification edges must not be mixed into established mathematical bridges or explicit reference-source links. An aggregate edge's `weight` must state whether it measures the number of entry-level connections, similarity, or a manually assigned weight. It must not be described as “theorem strength.”

## 3. Interpreting the two-dimensional map

Store coordinates separately as `x/y/layout_version/layout_method/seed`. Coordinate units are layout units. They do not represent difficulty, importance, mathematical or logical distance, the chronological order of knowledge, or proof cost. Separate connected components may have many connections in actual mathematics that the selected sources simply do not record.

An overview containing tens of thousands of nodes uses regions and hierarchical labels; the detailed index retains all islands. If the overview hides some edges, it must disclose the filtering rule and the total number of edges. A compressed two-dimensional map cannot preserve all high-dimensional adjacency. Search, adjacency lists, and shortest-path queries must therefore operate directly on the original graph; reasoning must not follow the visually shortest distance on the map. Path results must display their edge types. Paths through reference-source links serve only as reading routes.

## 4. Connecting to the two layers of MathCups tool classification

The first layer records what a tool produces: for example, a new representation, object, structure, invariant, estimate, algorithm, or proof mechanism. The second layer records discovery actions: for example, translation, revision driven by counterexamples, transfer by analogy, reformulation of a goal, composition, or passage from local to global. Only field semantics are specified here. The old MathCups registry has not been restored in the current workspace, so new K/M/D identifiers must not be invented and presented as compatible with it.

`typed_annotations` may contain two independent, progressively refined label intervals, `artifact_type` and `discovery_action_type`, each with `registry_version/must/may/open/evidence_refs/reviewer`. Without an individual review, the default is `must: [], may: [], open: true`. `must` is an evidence-supported lower bound. When `open=false`, `may` is the permitted upper bound; when `open=true`, `may` contains only candidate hints, and the permitted upper bound remains the entire fixed registry.

For a fixed universe of labels U, the interpretation is γ(L,H)={S⊆U | L⊆S⊆H}. Refinement must satisfy L_old⊆L_new and H_new⊆H_old. Adding a guessed label is not necessarily a refinement; narrowing an interval does not guarantee that its labels are true; and a historical account cannot be treated as a fact about a person's mental process. Changing the registry requires an explicit migration and must not be confused with refinement within the same registry.

The conditions of a mathematical bridge are also structured proof obligations. Record the range of objects, local assumptions, construction steps, output, and failure boundaries separately. Only a route's own conditions, or conditions it explicitly inherits, may be used; assumptions cannot be borrowed from neighboring routes. Store the date when an established theory appeared and the evidence type for its discovery process separately. Mathematical connections do not establish historical causation.

## 5. From a map to executable research

A research request should produce a concrete transfer contract: the source tool, target problem, preserved structure, lost structure, missing conditions, a limited boundary case that can be falsified, and the general proposition that ultimately needs to be proved. Candidates may arise from shared invariants, representations in the same category, complementary estimates, or isomorphic structures in different fields. Once a candidate is identified, prior work must be searched. The absence of an edge in a reference source must not be mistaken for scholarly novelty.

A complete research cycle is: candidate bridge → alignment of conditions → derivation or counterexample → revision of the tool contract → independent checking → registration of literature or formal evidence. Failed attempts and counterexamples also yield reusable knowledge about boundaries. The first release of this atlas does not claim to perform this research process automatically.
